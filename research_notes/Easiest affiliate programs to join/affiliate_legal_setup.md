## 1. Which states require sports betting affiliates to register or be licensed (fees, timelines, CPA vs revenue share, small individuals)?

### Takeaway
How you are paid decides the tier in most regulated states. Flat-fee/CPA affiliates usually need a cheaper "vendor registration" (or nothing). Revenue-share affiliates need a costlier supplier/ancillary/"certified" license with background checks. The registration rules apply to the business doing the promoting, not by audience size, and none of the sources describes an exemption for small individuals. In practice, sportsbooks will not pay you in a state until you are registered there.

### Cited Findings
- General pattern: CPA/flat fee leads to cheap vendor registration (or nothing), and revenue share leads to a full vendor/supplier license. NJ, PA, CO and MI are most commonly cited as requiring registration before performance-based pay. — [iGB Affiliate](https://www.igbaffiliate.com/en/articles/regulation-compliance/the-licence-lowdown-which-us-states-are-worth-it-for-affiliates/); [Vantegris 2026 guide](https://vantegris.com/blog/us-affiliate-licence-state-by-state/); [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/) (snippets only)
- **New Jersey (DGE):** affiliates paid a flat fee (CPC, CPM, CPA) can hold a **Vendor Registration**. Affiliates paid a percentage of money wagered or deposited (revenue share) must be licensed as an **Ancillary Casino Service Industry Enterprise (CSIE)**. A master affiliate taking rev share on sub-affiliates also needs the ancillary CSIE license. — [Pokerfuse 2014](https://pokerfuse.com/news/law-and-regulation/2014-06-09-new-jersey-dge-announces-affiliate-licensing-policy/) **[STALE]**; [Ifrah Law](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/new-jersey-dge-releases-guidance-for-affiliate-marketers/)
  - NJ fee: the only figure found was a $2,000 ancillary application fee from a 2013 proposed-rule summary. — [Ifrah Law PDF 2013](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/06/pages-from-woglr-july-2013.pdf) **[STALE/UNVERIFIED]**
  - Recent practice: DGE orders show SharpLink (2024) and Oddschecker USA (Jan 2026) withdrawing ancillary applications after switching to CPA/fixed-fee deals, and SharpLink's filing fee was not refunded. — [NJ DGE ruling 2024](https://www.nj.gov/oag/ge/docs/Rulings/2024/may1_15/C7sharplink.pdf); [NJ DGE ruling 2026](https://www.nj.gov/oag/ge/docs/Rulings/2026/jan1_15/C14oddschecker.pdf) (snippet-level only)
- **Pennsylvania (PGCB):** flat-fee affiliates **register** as a gaming service provider, and revenue-share affiliates must become a **certified** gaming service provider. Per the snippet of the PGCB page, registration costs **$2,000 plus a $500 entity application fee plus $60 per qualifier**, and certification costs a **$2,500 application fee plus a $4,000 certification fee**. Both last 4 years. Third-party sources give different numbers ($2,500 registration; $5,000 certification total). Vendors may need fingerprinting. — [PGCB Gaming-Related Service Providers](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/licensing/gaming-related-service-providers-information); [PGCB certification form](https://gamingcontrolboard.pa.gov/sites/default/files/licensure/Gaming-Related%20Service%20Providers%20Applications/Gaming_Related_Gaming_Service_Provider_Certification_Application_Fillable.pdf); [Ifrah Law PA](https://www.ifrahlaw.com/ifrah-on-igaming/how-to-ensure-your-media-affiliate-follows-pennsylvania-law/) **[fee conflict]**
- **Michigan (MGCB):** flat-fee affiliates register as **vendors** with a **$200 nonrefundable fee**. Revenue-share affiliates need a **supplier license**, quoted at a $2,500 application/background fee, a $5,000 license fee and a $2,500 annual fee. One guide says the $200 figure may come from an older rule version. — [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/); [Vantegris](https://vantegris.com/blog/us-affiliate-licence-state-by-state/) **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Colorado (Division of Gaming):** CPA affiliates need a **Vendor Minor** license (a simple form and a **$350** fee). Revenue-share affiliates need a **Vendor Major** license ($1,200 fee, a $10,000 background-check deposit, and investigators billed at $77/hr). — [Szilaghi](https://szilaghi.com/media-affiliate-gaming-license-cost-in-the-us/); [Legal 500 CO](https://www.legal500.com/guides/chapter/united-states-colorado-gambling-law/?export-pdf=); [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/)
- **Virginia (Lottery):** sports betting affiliates must register as **vendors**. The fee is **$500**, and the license lasts 3 years and renews for $500. — [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/); [Szilaghi](https://szilaghi.com/media-affiliate-gaming-license-cost-in-the-us/)
- **Arizona (ADG):** affiliates reportedly must be licensed. Guides cite **$1,500 initial and $500 renewal**, but one 2026 guide says those figures come only from secondary sources. The process starts with an ADG inquiry form. — [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/); [Vantegris](https://vantegris.com/blog/us-affiliate-licence-state-by-state/) **[UNVERIFIED]**

- **Ohio (OCCC):** a 2022 law-firm analysis said affiliate licensing was "doubtful", but affiliates must follow the commission's ad standards. - **Illinois:** one guide says IL (and Missouri) are **CPA-only** markets, meaning revenue share is banned. Another lists IL as "no license necessary". — [Vantegris](https://vantegris.com/blog/us-affiliate-licence-state-by-state/); [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/) **[CONFLICT]**

### Inferences
- **Practical tiering for a beginner:** take **CPA/flat-fee deals only**. That keeps you in the cheap vendor-registration tier (roughly $200 MI, $350 CO, $500 VA; PA is a notable exception at about $2,500 even for registration) and avoids background-check licenses ($5k to $10k+).
- **Easiest states (lowest friction), per secondary sources:** states reported to require nothing for affiliates, namely NY (for CPA), OH, IL (CPA), and possibly IN after July 2025 and TN. All of these are unverified or conflicting. The costliest states for a small individual are **PA** (registration alone about $2.5k) and any revenue-share license.
- The affiliate is typically the one that must register, and in some states that can be an individual or sole proprietor. Most applications are designed for entities with "qualifiers", so forming an LLC may simplify filings. This is an inference, not sourced.
- Many sportsbook affiliate programs (and sub-affiliate networks) handle state registration requirements by only paying for players in states where the affiliate is registered. Confirm this in each program's T&Cs. This is an inference.
- Timelines: no reliable timeline data was found (see Gaps).

## 2. DFS and prediction-market affiliates: state registration? Sweepstakes affiliate legal risk under 2025–2026 bans?

### Takeaway
DFS affiliate registration is lightly regulated: secondary sources say few or no states require it, though one names NJ, NY and MA. Prediction-market (Kalshi-type) promotion currently runs through referral programs, not formal affiliate licensing, with fluctuating excluded-state lists. **Sweepstakes casinos are now the highest legal risk**: several 2025–2026 state bans explicitly reach "promoters" and "media affiliates", with criminal penalties in CA, OK and LA.

### Cited Findings
- DFS: one 2026 operator guide says DFS affiliate registration is required in **NJ, NY and MA** and not elsewhere. A separate affiliate guide says regulators "do not currently require affiliates promoting daily fantasy, horse racing, sweepstakes casinos, and online lotteries to register or license." — [track360 DFS playbook](https://track360.io/blog/daily-fantasy-sports-operator-affiliate-playbook-2026); [BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/affiliate/) **[CONFLICT/UNVERIFIED]**
- Kalshi uses a **referral (refer-a-friend) program**, not a formal affiliate program, per search summaries. Sports event contracts are reported restricted in **MI, NV and WA** as of Oct 26, 2026, and a Nevada court ruling reaffirmed the need for a state gaming license. Referral-ineligible state lists conflict (one lists AZ, IL, MA, MD, MI, MT, NV, OH, WA). — [CBS Sports prediction-market legality](https://www.cbssports.com/prediction/news/prediction-market-legal-states/); [Sports Handle Kalshi refer-a-friend](https://sportshandle.com/kalshi/refer-a-friend/); [iGamingFuture](https://igamingfuture.com/prediction-markets/news/kalshi-referral-code/) **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **California AB 831** (effective Jan 1, 2026): bans sweepstakes casinos and makes it unlawful for payment processors, platform providers and **media affiliates** to knowingly and willfully support or promote them. A violation is a **misdemeanor, up to 1 year in jail, and up to $25,000 per violation**. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/gaming/californias-ab-831-bans-sweepstakes-casinos-expands-liability-vendors/)
- **Oklahoma SB 1589** (effective Nov 1, 2026, after a veto override) covers promoters and media affiliates. Sources conflict on the penalty (Class C2 felony vs. a felony with $500–$2,000 fines). — [CasinoReports](https://www.casinoreports.com/news/sweepstakes-ban-2026-state-roundup/); [InfoLawGroup](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2026/7/27/sweepstakes-casino-laws-in-2026-new-bans-proposed-legislation-and-regulatory-trends) **[CONFLICT]**
- **Louisiana HB 883 and HB 53** (effective Aug 1, 2026): up to $20,000 and 5 years for promoters who knowingly support the operation, doubled if someone under 21 is involved. HB 53 brings these offenses under racketeering law, which can reach affiliates. — [CasinoReports](https://www.casinoreports.com/news/sweepstakes-ban-2026-state-roundup/); [InfoLawGroup](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2026/7/27/sweepstakes-casino-laws-in-2026-new-bans-proposed-legislation-and-regulatory-trends)
- **New York S5935A**: $10,000–$100,000 per violation, and covers knowing support by "media affiliates". **Maine LD 2007**: $10,000–$100,000 civil fines (effective about mid-July 2026). **Indiana HB 1052**: $100,000 civil penalty per violation, targeting operators, suppliers and **promoters**. **Tennessee SB 2136**: treated as a deceptive practice under the Consumer Protection Act. **Iowa SF 2289**: cease-and-desist and injunction powers. **Massachusetts**: no ban in force (H 4431 was sent to study in March 2026). — [CasinoReports](https://www.casinoreports.com/news/sweepstakes-ban-2026-state-roundup/); [InfoLawGroup](https://www.infolawgroup.com/insights/2026/7/27/sweepstakes-casino-laws-in-2026-new-bans-proposed-legislation-and-regulatory-trends); [Gambling.com map](https://www.gambling.com/us/sweepstakes-casinos/legal)

### Inferences
- A beginner should **avoid sweepstakes-casino affiliate links entirely**, or at minimum never promote them to anyone in CA, NY, LA, OK (from Nov 1, 2026), ME, IN, TN or other banning states. A group chat or Discord cannot reliably geo-restrict, so the friends-and-Discord model makes this risk hard to control.
- Prediction-market promotion carries regulatory uncertainty because of ongoing state-versus-CFTC litigation. Treat it as a referral program governed by the platform's terms, and avoid restricted states.
- DFS is probably the lowest-friction gaming vertical, but check NJ, NY and MA if you have players there.

## 3. FTC endorsement and disclosure rules for affiliate links (Discord, social, websites)

### Takeaway
The FTC's 2023 Endorsement Guides require a "clear and conspicuous" disclosure (difficult to miss and easily understandable) of any material connection, including commissions. "#ad" or "Paid link" next to the link works. "Affiliate link" alone does not. These rules are platform-neutral, so they apply to Discord and group-chat posts too.

### Cited Findings
- The 2023 update defines "clear and conspicuous" as "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers." — [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-2); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/ftc-issues-updated-guidance-on-endorsements-compliance)
- "Ad", "Paid ad" and "#ad" remain acceptable. "Affiliate link" or "buy now" alone is **not sufficient**, and "commissionable link" is not a clear disclosure. A "paid link" label placed clearly beside the link is acceptable per the FAQ summaries. — [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-updates-faqs-for-endorsement-guides-offers-more-guidance-on-social-media-and-video-endorsements); [Locke Lord](https://lockelord.com/newsandevents/publications/2023/07/quickstudy-ftc-endorsement-guide)
- "Liking", "pinning" or "sharing" a link can be an endorsement if done as part of a sponsored campaign. Advertisers are liable for deceptive endorsements even when the endorser is not. — [Manatt](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/advertising-law/an-in-depth-look-at-the-ftcs-updates-2)
- For financial products, sources say the FTC Endorsement Guides apply to affiliates. The CFPB's UDAAP authority covers deceptive marketing, and the CFPB evaluates the ad together with the landing page and sign-up flow. Credit-product promotion may trigger TILA disclosures. — [Advertise Purple](https://www.advertisepurple.com/?p=11713); [Sedric](https://www.sedric.ai/blog/ensuring-compliance-in-affiliate-and-partner-marketing-for-financial-services-a-strategic-guide) (vendor blogs, **[UNVERIFIED]**)

### Inferences
- Practical wording: put "**#ad — I earn a commission if you sign up**" or "Paid link" in the same message as the link, before it, in every Discord or group-chat post. Pin a disclosure in the channel as a supplement, not a substitute. On a website, add a disclosure near the links plus a site-wide disclosure page.
- Sending a referral to friends still counts. The "material connection" rule doesn't depend on audience size.
- Fintech (credit cards, loans): don't paraphrase APRs or terms yourself. Use the issuer-provided copy, since issuers usually require approved creatives. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No Discord-specific FTC guidance was found. The FTC's own "FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking" page was not readable (fetch blocked).
- Current FTC civil-penalty-per-violation figures were not verified.
- The CFPB's post-2025 enforcement posture on affiliates was not verified.

## 4. Responsible-gaming advertising rules (21+, "risk-free" bans, hotline messaging, no college/under-21 targeting)

### Takeaway
The AGA Responsible Marketing Code (current version September 2025) bans "risk-free" language, bars college partnerships, and requires featured people to be 21+. It also allows social, SMS and web marketing only where at least **73.6%** of the audience is reasonably expected to be 21+. AGA members must give the Code to their affiliates. States add mandatory problem-gambling messaging (such as 1-800-GAMBLER) and penalties for misleading "free" claims.

### Cited Findings
- AGA Code (2023 revision): **"risk-free" is banned**, college partnerships promoting sports wagering are prohibited (exceptions for alumni networks and RG content), and **anyone featured in ads must be 21+**. — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/109587/aga-tightens-up-sports-betting-ad-responsible-marketing-code/); [Klein Moynihan Turco](https://kleinmoynihan.com/aga-updates-code-to-enhance-sports-wagering-marketing-restrictions/); [AGA Marketing Code page](https://americangaming.org/marketing-code/)
- Sept 2025 Code text (partial snippet): AGA members "will deliver a copy of this Code to advertising agencies, media buyers, **affiliates**." Social, SMS, website and streaming marketing is allowed only where **≥73.6% of the audience is reasonably expected to be 21+**, and the Code is reviewed annually. — [AGA Responsible Gaming Code of Conduct (Sept 2025) PDF](https://www.americangaming.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Responsible-Gaming-Code-of-Conduct-2026.pdf) (snippet only, full text not read)
- **New Jersey** reportedly requires 1-800-GAMBLER on every ad and bans targeting minors. — [oddsindex](https://oddsindex.com/guides/sports-betting-advertising-rules) **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Colorado** requires "Gambling problem? Call or TEXT 1-800-GAMBLER" on all media advertising, and operators must ensure it appears **directly or through their marketing affiliates**. — [Colorado SB Rule 9 (Jan 2026)](https://sbg.colorado.gov/sites/sbg/files/documents/SBRule-9-011426.pdf)
- **Massachusetts** prohibits calling a promotion "free" if users must risk their own money and requires a problem-gambling helpline message. MGC fined Penn Sports Interactive $25,000 in 2023 over its "Can't Lose Parlay" ad. — [The Regulatory Review](https://www.theregreview.org/2025/09/06/seminar-how-should-regulators-address-sports-betting-advertisements/); [oddsindex](https://oddsindex.com/guides/sports-betting-advertising-rules)
- **New York** (2023) adopted stricter ad rules, including no ads on college campuses, plus the affiliate compensation limits noted in section 1. — [Sports Handle](https://sportshandle.com/new-york-gaming-commission-stricter-advertising-rules/)
- **Maine** bars sports wagering ads on college campuses aimed primarily at under-21 audiences. — [Maine C.M.R. 16-633 ch. 64 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/maine/C-M-R-16-633-ch-64)
- A state-by-state compendium of online sports betting ad regulations exists (NCPG/Vixio, Sept 2024). — [NCPG/Vixio PDF](https://www.ncpgambling.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/NCPG_Vixio-U.S.-States-Online-Sports-Betting-Regulations.pdf)

### Inferences
- Minimum compliance copy on every post: **"21+ (18+ for some DFS states). Gambling problem? Call or text 1-800-GAMBLER. Must be physically present in [state]. Terms apply."** Never say "risk-free", "free bet" (unless no deposit is needed), "can't lose" or "guaranteed".
- Audience age is a real issue for a friends-and-Discord model. If any friends or Discord members are under 21 or in college, the AGA 73.6% audience standard and state rules make promotion to that group problematic. Gate the channel to verified 21+ members.
- Operators push these rules to affiliates via contract, and violations can mean clawed-back commissions or termination even where the state doesn't regulate the affiliate directly.

### Gaps
- The full Sept 2025 AGA Code text was not read, so specific changes from the 2023 version are unconfirmed.
- Ohio-specific ad rules and penalties were not confirmed from a primary source.
- Mandatory hotline numbers vary by state (some use state-specific lines). Not verified state by state.

## 5. Tax: affiliate income reporting, self-employment tax, LLC?

### Takeaway
Affiliate commissions are taxable income whether or not you receive a 1099. For payments made in 2026, the 1099-NEC/MISC issuance threshold rose from $600 to **$2,000** under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, and it will be indexed for inflation from 2027. An LLC is not legally required to be an affiliate, but it may help with state vendor filings and liability separation.

### Cited Findings
- The 1099-NEC/MISC threshold rises from $600 to **$2,000** for payments made in 2026, first reported in the 2027 filing season. The $600 threshold still applies to tax year 2025. It will be inflation-indexed from 2027. The basis is OBBBA (P.L. 119-21) §70433, which amends IRC §6041. — [Patriot Software](https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/); [TaxBandits](https://blog.taxbandits.com/form-1099-nec-instructions-for-2026-tax-year-a-complete-guide/); [OnPay](https://onpay.com/insights/1099-reporting-threshold-updates/)
- "All income remains taxable, even if it's not reported on a 1099 form." — [Patriot Software](https://www.patriotsoftware.com/blog/accounting/1099-reporting-threshold/)
- 1099-K: one source says OBBBA reverted the threshold to **$20,000 and 200 transactions**, and another says the 1099-K threshold did not change. — [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/19/obbba-1099-nec-misc-threshold-2000-small-business-guide); [TaxBandits](https://blog.taxbandits.com/form-1099-nec-instructions-for-2026-tax-year-a-complete-guide/) **[CONFLICT]**

### Inferences
- From general tax knowledge, **not sourced in this session (verify with IRS or a CPA)**: affiliate commissions are usually self-employment income reported on Schedule C. Self-employment tax (about 15.3%, Schedule SE) generally applies once net SE earnings reach $400. Quarterly estimated tax may be needed. Expenses (domain, hosting, registration fees) are generally deductible.
- On an LLC: a single-member LLC is a disregarded entity for federal tax (no tax savings by itself). Its value lies in liability separation and in some state gaming forms expecting an entity. Weigh state LLC fees (for example, California's $800 annual franchise tax) against expected earnings of a 38-person audience. This is an inference.
- Affiliate programs will collect a W-9 (SSN or EIN). Getting a free EIN avoids sharing your SSN. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No primary IRS source was read on SE tax or the treatment of affiliate income.
- State income tax and state business-license requirements were not researched.

## 6. Minimum setup affiliate managers expect; does a Discord/group-chat-only approach get approved?

### Takeaway
No source found published minimum traffic thresholds. Applications typically ask for name, email, website or channel info and payment details, and reviews take a few business days. A website is "beneficial but not strictly necessary" per one guide, but no source confirmed that any sportsbook program approves Discord-only applicants. Regulated sportsbook programs in particular tend to require a compliant web presence and state registration.

### Cited Findings
- The typical process is: application, approval, tracking links and creatives. One example form asks for name, email, **website information** and payment details, with review in "a few business days." — [RichAds beginner guide 2026](https://richads.com/blog/how-to-become-a-sports-betting-affiliate-2026/)
- "While having a website is beneficial, it's not strictly necessary"; affiliates use social media, forums and other channels. — [RichAds](https://richads.com/blog/how-to-become-a-sports-betting-affiliate-2026/) (single secondary source)
- Recommended path: pick one regulated geo, join a licensed sportsbook program, learn its compliance rules, run a small test, then scale. Choose CPA, rev share or hybrid deliberately. — [RichAds](https://richads.com/blog/how-to-become-a-sports-betting-affiliate-2026/)
- Operators must submit affiliate tracking tools and marketing for regulator review in some states (for example TN). — [tennessee.bet](https://www.tennessee.bet/about-us/gambling-regulations/) **[UNVERIFIED]**

### Inferences
- A practical minimum setup to improve approval odds:
  1. A one-page website on your own domain with an About section, the content and audience description, **21+ and responsible-gaming footer (1-800-GAMBLER)**, an **affiliate disclosure page**, a **privacy policy** and a contact email.
  2. At least one public social profile (X, Instagram or TikTok) that shows the niche.
  3. A short traffic plan: who the audience is (with ages), where they are (which legal states), how you'll promote, and the expected volume.
  4. Business details: legal name or LLC, W-9/EIN, payout method.
  5. State vendor registration in the target state if the program requires it.
- A Discord-only or group-chat-only applicant with about 38 people is likely to be rejected by major regulated sportsbook programs (FanDuel, DraftKings and similar), or limited to their **consumer refer-a-friend programs**. For a 38-person audience, those referral programs are the realistic, low-compliance starting point: no vendor registration is reported as needed, though FTC disclosure and 21+ rules still apply. Low-threshold networks or DFS and fintech programs may be more accepting. This is an inference, not sourced.
- Discord is also a weak fit for compliance because you can't verify that members are 21+ or physically in a legal state, which matters under AGA and state rules.
